Welcome to Digidream

Sectors · sprites · 2D and 3D

Build retro worlds in small sessions.

Digidream is a pocket studio for sector-based games. Draw rooms, paint textures, rig sprites, make sounds and music, wire up the logic, and press Play, all on your phone.

Did you know... a whole room fits in ten spare minutes: draw it in plan, set its floor and ceiling, then walk it in 3D.

Why I made this

Being a huge fan of sector-based and early 2.5D/3D games, I always felt that modern engines were somewhat overkill for that style. As a teenager I spent hours in tools like Doom Builder and the Warcraft III editor. Later I learned programming and even worked briefly in a game studio, but life eventually led me into another profession.

Making games stayed one of my biggest hobbies, though. With a full-time job and other responsibilities, free time became a privilege you snatch between other things, and even then I often spent it scrolling.

So at some point I decided to quickly make a little tool for building levels for a game that didn't exist. Doing it all myself would have taken too much of that free time, so AI helped, at the cost of performance and stability in places. Then came a sprite editor, a texture editor, and so on.

Here I'm publishing early versions of these tools and the studio that combines them, so that someone else might have fun with them too.

  1. One short session is enough for:
  2. One roomDraw it in plan, set floor and ceiling, walk it in 3D.
  3. One texturePaint a 64 × 64 wall with a palette and see it tile.
  4. One soundShape a hit or a door from a recipe, then hear it in game.
  5. One monsterDraw its brain as states: look, chase, attack, pain.

    AI-slopped examples

    These two example games are AI-slopped. AI agents built them by driving the studio, as a test that the tools hold up, with no engine changes. They show what the tools can do. They are not good games, and I didn't make them.

    Gem Hollow

    A platformer: one level in four zones, levers, gates, gems, slimes and bees. Art from Kenney (CC0). It comes with the studio: Home → Gem Hollow.

    Cinderfall

    A first-person shooter in a smelting colony: five districts, no loading screens, one boss. Textures and music from Freedoom.

    System Properties

    Under the floor

    A deterministic, fixed-point C++ core. The same simulation code runs natively on the server and as WebAssembly in the browser, so every machine computes the same world.

    Core

    Fixed-point C++ simulation: sectors, bodies, movers, triangulation. No floats in the sim, so arm64, x86-64 and WASM agree bit for bit.

    Rendering

    three.js on WebGL. Sector meshes are baked by the map compiler; sprites stand in 3D as billboards; skies, fog and weather per sector.

    Gameplay

    LuaJIT scripts on the server, or Blueprint-style graphs authored in the studio, which compile to the same calls.

    Network

    Server-authoritative, with client-side prediction and delta-encoded snapshots, carried over WebTransport (HTTP/3, QUIC) through a small Node edge.

    Audio

    DigidreamSynth, a wavetable and subtractive MIDI synth, and DigidreamSound, sound effects as recipes. Both fixed-point: the same samples on every platform.

    Packaging

    The Packager assembles prebuilt engine kits in the browser: an Android APK, and Windows, macOS and Linux apps on Electron or Tauri.

    Multiplayer is built into the engine from the start, but the studio does not expose it yet: games made in it are single-player for now.
